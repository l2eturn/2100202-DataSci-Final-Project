What is this project about?

We used real Brazilian e-commerce data to answer business questions that any online store would care about — who are the best customers, why do reviews go bad, and can we predict problems before they happen?

Real Business Data

Pandas Skills

4 Required Pandas Techniques

All 4 mandatory operations were implemented correctly across the pipeline.

🧹

1. Data Cleansing

Converted 5 timestamp columns from string to datetime. Filtered to delivered orders only. Filled 57,000+ missing review comments with "(no comment)".

df[col] = pd.to_datetime(df[col]) df["review_comment_message"] .fillna("(no comment)")
🔗

2. Merge / Join

Chained 6 pd.merge() calls to combine all 7 tables into one master DataFrame using left joins on order_id, customer_id, and product_id.

df = orders .merge(customers, on="customer_id") .merge(items, on="order_id") .merge(reviews, on="order_id")
📦

3. Groupby + Aggregation

Built an RFM table by grouping per customer — computing Recency (last purchase), Frequency (order count), and Monetary (total spend).

rfm = df.groupby("customer_id") .agg( recency = ("date", "max"), frequency = ("order_id", "nunique"), monetary = ("payment", "sum") )
📋

4. Pivot Table

Created a category × month revenue matrix — rows are the top-10 product categories, columns are months, values are total sales in BRL.

pivot = df.pivot_table( index = "category", columns = "order_month", values = "price", aggfunc = "sum" )
Research Questions & Answers

4 Business Questions, Answered by Data

Each question was answered using actual numbers from the dataset — not assumptions.

Q1

Who are the high-value customers and how do they behave?

We computed an RFM score for every unique customer — how recently they bought (Recency), how many times (Frequency), and how much they spent (Monetary). Then K-Means grouped them into 4 segments.

Answer: The largest group is At Risk — customers who bought once and disappeared. VIP customers are rare but drive disproportionate revenue. Regular buyers form the reliable backbone of the platform.

Q2

What factors actually affect the review score?

We ran a correlation analysis across all numeric variables and then confirmed the findings using Random Forest feature importance.

Answer: Delivery time is by far the most important factor (r = -0.313). The longer an order takes to arrive, the lower the review. Price has almost zero correlation with satisfaction — customers don't care how much they paid, they care when it showed up.

Q3

Can we predict which orders will receive a bad review?

We trained a Random Forest Classifier to predict "Satisfied (4–5 ★)" vs "Not Satisfied (1–3 ★)" for each order, using delivery time, delay, price, freight cost, and product category as inputs.

Answer: Yes — the model achieves 76% overall accuracy. It correctly identifies 14,233 satisfied customers and 2,552 unhappy ones. The main weakness is that it misses ~half of the unhappy customers (because 76% of the training data was already "satisfied", causing the model to lean that way).

Q4

Which product categories generate the most revenue, and what is the trend?

Revenue was aggregated by category and by month to answer both the ranking and the trend.

Answer: health_beauty is the top revenue category at 1.23M BRL, followed by watches_gifts and bed_bath_table. Overall revenue grew from near zero in late 2016 to a peak of 1.55M BRL/month in November 2017 (Black Friday), then stabilized at 1.2–1.5M BRL/month through 2018.

Machine Learning

Two ML Models, Two Different Goals

Both models use sklearn Pipelines — preprocessing and the model are bundled together as a single object.

🔵 Unsupervised Learning

K-Means Clustering

Pipeline: StandardScaler → KMeans(k=4)

Goal: Find natural customer groups without any labels — the algorithm discovers the patterns by itself.

  • Scaled RFM features so all 3 dimensions have equal weight
  • Used the Elbow Method to confirm K=4 is optimal
  • Labeled each cluster based on its average RFM profile

🌳 Supervised Learning

Random Forest Classifier

Pipeline: SimpleImputer → StandardScaler → RandomForestClassifier

Goal: Predict satisfaction label (satisfied / not satisfied) using order features.

  • 200 decision trees, max depth 10, class_weight="balanced"
  • 80/20 train-test split with stratification
  • Top features: delay_days (38%), delivery_days (27%)
